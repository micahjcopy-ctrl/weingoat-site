The short version

How I learned to stop guessing.

My dad played, so I knew what a flush was before I knew what a range was. In tenth and eleventh grade a few of us built a home game that grew to thirty or forty people, and with my teammate Noah and my friend Tanner I got obsessed. Not with winning. With the process of getting better and finding edges. Every three months I'd look back and realize how little the player from three months ago knew.

The night that changed the most was a loss. $5,000 in one session, a serious piece of my bankroll at the time, and for the first time I didn't trust my game. I didn't play for over a month. What brought me back was the trunk of the tree: lower stakes, the fundamentals, A, B and C. A month later I was back at my normal stakes, and the week that followed didn't feel real in the other direction. Same decisions, opposite results. That's variance, and it taught me the only rule I still play by: if I played my best, I don't care about the result.

The turnaround wasn't a secret line. It was buying into the process, asking better players a lot of questions, and accepting that I'm a poker player. That's what I do. The results changed when the identity did.

I read, I journal, I listen to long-form content, and I still watch more poker than is reasonable. I can read a player's body language, but I'll tell you the same thing I tell every student: live reads come second to strategy and application. That's what the Playbook, the community and the coaching are for.
